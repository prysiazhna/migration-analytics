## Project Context

As a Ukrainian immigrant, I was personally motivated to explore and visualize migration trends to gain a deeper understanding of how recent global events have affected millions of Ukrainians, including myself.
This dashboard presents a visual overview of Ukrainian migration over the past 15 years, with a particular focus on the period that began with the full-scale Russian invasion in 2022.

## Business Questions
- How has Ukraine’s net migration pattern changed over time, particularly after 2022
- Which countries host the largest share of Ukrainian refugees
- How concentrated refugee flows are across destination countries
- How migration dynamics differ before and after the full-scale invasion  

## Tools & Stack
- Microsoft Excel — data preparation, aggregation, formulas, PivotTables and dashboard construction
- Tableau Public — interactive visualizations, parameter-driven KPIs and cross-filtered dashboards

## Data Sources
- World Bank — net migration statistics
- UNHCR — refugee distribution and country-level refugee counts
- Additional verified public datasets related to post-2022 displacement

## Dashboard Structure & Key Views

### Refugee Distribution by Country  
An interactive map and aggregated distribution view highlight how Ukrainian refugees are concentrated across destination countries, with clear differentiation between top host countries and the rest of the world.

### Net Migration Trends
Time-series views compare long-term net migration patterns with post-2022 dynamics, making the structural break caused by the war clearly visible.

### Country-Level Analysis
Country selection dynamically updates key indicators such as total refugee count and relative ranking, enabling direct comparison across destinations.

## Insights
- Refugee distribution is highly concentrated, with a small group of countries hosting the majority of displaced Ukrainians, largely driven by geographic proximity and policy accessibility.
- Net migration trends show a clear structural break after 2022, indicating a shift from gradual migration to large-scale forced displacement driven by geopolitical events.
- Pre-2022 migration patterns were relatively stable, while post-2022 flows dominate historical comparisons and significantly alter long-term averages.


## Summary
This project applies a structured analytical approach to examine long-term Ukrainian migration patterns and the structural shift caused by the 2022 full-scale invasion.

By combining curated public datasets with interactive dashboards in Excel and Tableau, the analysis translates complex migration statistics into a clear, interpretable narrative focused on scale, concentration and structural change.
